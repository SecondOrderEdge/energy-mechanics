The Strait of Hormuz carries ~21 mmbpd of crude and condensate plus ~5 mmbpd of refined products — together roughly 30% of seaborne oil trade. Every Gulf producer (Saudi Arabia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain) depends on it. Three pipelines bypass the strait — Saudi East-West, UAE Habshan-Fujairah, and the idle Iraq-Turkey Kirkuk-Ceyhan line — with combined nameplate capacity ~7 mmbpd, well under half of normal Hormuz volume. Roughly ~14 mmbpd has no detour.

~25% of global LNG transits Hormuz at full flow — almost entirely Qatari output from Ras Laffan. Japan, South Korea, and parts of China are highly dependent; a sustained closure forces buyers to bid up Atlantic-basin LNG (US, Trinidad, West Africa) and pulls cargoes away from European storage refill.
