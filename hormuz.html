The Strait of Hormuz carries ~21 mmbpd of crude and condensate plus ~5 mmbpd of refined products — together roughly 30% of seaborne oil trade. Every Gulf producer (Saudi Arabia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain) depends on it. Three pipelines bypass the strait — Saudi East-West to Yanbu on the Red Sea, UAE Habshan-Fujairah to the Gulf of Oman, and the idle Iraq-Turkey Kirkuk-Ceyhan line — with combined nameplate capacity ~7 mmbpd, well under half of normal Hormuz volume. Roughly ~14 mmbpd has no detour.

Recent timeline · key events

JUN 4, 2026 Geopolitical risk premium reflected across Brent and WTI. Tanker insurance war-risk rates reported elevated. No confirmed strait closure.
MAY 2026 Iran/Israel escalation cycle resumes; missile + drone exchanges. No tanker incidents reported in the strait itself.
APR 2024 Iran seizes MSC Aries near strait (commercial vessel, container ship). Symbolic interruption of strait freedom-of-navigation norm.
JAN 2024– Houthi missile and drone attacks on Red Sea shipping force most tankers to detour via Cape of Good Hope, adding 10–14 days transit. Bab el-Mandeb traffic down ~70%.
2023 Iraq–Turkey Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline (~1.6 mmbpd nameplate) shut down after arbitration ruling against Turkey. Removes one bypass option.
2019 Tanker attacks in Gulf of Oman near strait approach; Iran shoots down US RQ-4 drone. Brent jumps ~$5/bbl.
1984–88 Tanker War during Iran-Iraq conflict — 451 ships attacked, 259 disabled or sunk. Strait remained open throughout; convoy escort protected most flows.
